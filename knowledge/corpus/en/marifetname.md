# Marifetname — Islamic cosmology, the elements, temperament and firasa

*Marifetname*, completed by Erzurumlu İbrahim Hakkı in 1757, gathers the
astronomy, medicine, anatomy and ethics of the Islamic world into a single
structure. Its astrological backbone comes from the same source as the
Ptolemaic tradition, but it departs from it in three places, and those three
add depth this app did not have: **the rulers of the days and hours**, **the
mansions of the Moon**, and **firasa** — the method of reading inward
disposition from outward sign.

## The Four Elements and Their Qualities

Beneath every reading lie four elements, each holding two qualities. An
element is not a substance but a **tendency**: what heats, what dries, what
holds, what disperses.

**Fire:** hot and dry. Rising, catching, spreading fast. It does not stay in
its place; it is quick to take a shape and quick to break one.

**Air:** hot and moist. Spreading, carrying, passing between. Its work is to
clear and to ease; when it is spoiled, what it carries is spoiled too.

**Water:** cold and moist. Gathering, dissolving, taking shape. It forms to
its vessel without losing its own nature.

**Earth:** cold and dry. Standing, holding, preserving. Its weight comes from
its stillness; it is the element slowest to accept change.

Elements that share a quality can pass into one another; those that share
none (fire and water, air and earth) cannot convert directly. A missing
element in a chart is not the absence of that tendency but a sign that
reaching it runs through another element.

## The Four Humours: The Temperaments

The four elements have their counterparts in the body as four humours, and
their proportion gives a person's temperament. Temperament is not an
identity but a **point of balance**: it shows where someone runs to excess
and where they run short.

**Sanguine (blood) — hot and moist, answering to air.** Mobile, sociable,
quick to warm. Out of balance it scatters: it moves to the next thing before
finishing the last.

**Choleric (yellow bile) — hot and dry, answering to fire.** Sharp, fast,
decided. Out of balance it burns: anger and haste come from here.

**Melancholic (black bile) — cold and dry, answering to earth.** Deep,
patient, heavy. Out of balance it closes: worry and withdrawal come from here.

**Phlegmatic — cold and moist, answering to water.** Calm, sustaining,
conciliatory. Out of balance it stalls: delay and non-response come from here.

Nobody is a single temperament; all four are present in everyone and one or
two come forward. What a reading should ask is not "which type are you" but
**in which direction do you lose your balance**.

## The Signs, Their Elements, Months and Seasons

The division into twelve is not a feature of the heavens but of the year
itself: twelve signs answer to twelve months, four triplicities to four
seasons. The Sun travels roughly one degree a day and crosses one sign a
month.

The year begins at Aries; when the Sun enters it, night and day equalise and
the day begins to lengthen.

- **Spring:** Aries, Taurus, Gemini
- **Summer:** Cancer, Leo, Virgo
- **Autumn:** Libra, Scorpio, Sagittarius
- **Winter:** Capricorn, Aquarius, Pisces

The temperament of the air changes with the season as well: spring temperate,
summer hot, autumn near-temperate, winter cold. Physicians treat this as
varying by climate; astrologers tie it to the Sun's sign — that is, the
season is measured by the sky, not by the calendar.

## The Natures of the Seven Planets

The planets are ordered in seven spheres, Saturn furthest and the Moon
nearest. The order is not only distance but **speed**: the far one moves
slowly, the near one quickly. The longer a planet takes to do its work, the
longer the period its influence marks.

**Saturn (Zuhal)** — cold and dry. Furthest, heaviest. It bounds, delays and
consolidates. What it builds comes slowly and does not break easily.

**Jupiter (Musteri)** — hot and moist, temperate. It widens, makes fruitful,
sets order. This is the tradition's **greater benefic**.

**Mars (Merih)** — hot and dry. It cuts, separates, hastens. It stays about
forty days in a sign when direct, about two months when retrograde. The
tradition's **lesser malefic**.

**Sun (Shams)** — hot and dry, measured. It makes visible, it centres. A
planet it draws close to is hidden inside its light.

**Venus (Zuhre)** — temperate, with moisture predominating. It harmonises,
beautifies, brings near. The tradition's **lesser benefic**.

**Mercury (Utarit)** — variable; it both dries and moistens. It never moves
far from the Sun. It takes on the nature of whatever planet it stands beside.

**Moon (Kamer)** — cold and moist. The fastest of them. It carries what it
receives rather than storing it; it makes everything temporary.

**A note on benefic and malefic:** the tradition counts Jupiter and Venus
fortunate, Saturn and Mars unfortunate. But the distinction is not moral, it
is one of **measure**: what is temperate nourishes, what is excessive strains.
Saturn's cold is patience in one place and rigidity in another. A planet does
not press because it is bad in itself, but because it has lost the measure.

## The Rulers of the Days and Hours

This is one of Marifetname's most concrete contributions, and it can still be
calculated today.

**Every day has a ruler:**

| Day | Ruler |
|---|---|
| Sunday | Sun |
| Monday | Moon |
| Tuesday | Mars |
| Wednesday | Mercury |
| Thursday | Jupiter |
| Friday | Venus |
| Saturday | Saturn |

**The hours are ruled in turn as well.** The day is divided into twelve hours
and the night into twelve, and each hour is ruled by a planet. The order runs
from slowest to fastest:

> Saturn → Jupiter → Mars → Sun → Venus → Mercury → Moon → (repeat)

The **first hour of the day belongs to that day's ruler**. On Sunday the first
hour is the Sun's, the second Venus's, the third Mercury's, and so on around
the cycle.

**These hours are not of equal length.** The arc of the day is divided into
twelve and the arc of the night into twelve separately. On a summer day an
hour of daylight runs longer than sixty minutes; on a winter day, shorter. The
"hour" here is a measure of **light**, not of the clock.

What this gives a reading: the day and hour in which something is begun show
under whose nature it started. It is a **ground**, not a verdict.

## The Mansions of the Moon

The Moon crosses the zodiac in about twenty-eight days and rests each day in
one "station". These twenty-eight stations are the **mansions of the Moon**,
each answering to a little over twelve degrees of the zodiac.

A mansion is a finer measure than a sign: a sign locates the Moon within a
thirty-degree slice, a mansion within about thirteen. Two different days in
the same sign are two different mansions.

The tradition gives each mansion a nature and a fitness — which works
(sowing, travelling, agreements, treatment) are well begun under it. The idea
worth keeping is this: **the Moon's condition is daily and fast.** It shows
the colour of the day, not the standing structure of a chart.

## The Order of the Spheres

The stars are of two kinds. The **fixed stars** never change their distances
from one another; they sit on the eighth sphere, and the twelve signs of the
zodiac are marked on that sphere. The **planets** draw nearer to and further
from one another; each has its own sphere.

The distinction still matters: in a chart the signs are a fixed ground and the
planets are markers moving across it. What changes in a reading comes from the
planets; what stands comes from the signs.

## Firasa: From Outward Sign to Temperament

*Firasa* (the science of physiognomy, *kiyafa*) is the method of passing from
outward appearance to inward state. Its logic is simple and rests on the
doctrine of temperament: body and disposition come from the same mixture, so
the balance of hot-cold and dry-moist visible in the body points the same way
in the character.

Firasa is therefore not a list of face types but a **reading back to quality**:

- Signs of heat (live colour, quick movement, full voice) → the choleric and
  sanguine side; quick to warm, quick to decide, quick to anger.
- Signs of cold (still colour, heavy movement, low voice) → the melancholic
  and phlegmatic side; slow to warm, slow to decide, long to hold.
- Signs of dryness (fine build, hard line, sharp edge) → not holding,
  separating, drawing boundaries.
- Signs of moisture (full build, soft line) → holding, adapting, struggling
  to let go.

## Facial Features and Their Temperamental Counterparts

The tradition assigns a tendency to each part of the face. What follows are
**signs**, not verdicts:

- **Forehead and head:** a broad forehead is counted toward openness and
  breadth of grasp, a narrow one toward concentration and narrowing.
- **Brows and the region of the eyes:** an open, even line, an open manner;
  a knitted, dense line, speech held back.
- **Nose:** a long nose is counted toward a heavy, distanced grasp, a short
  one toward quickness and caution; a bent line toward resolve and holding a
  direction.
- **Mouth and lips:** a large mouth toward boldness and outwardness, a small
  one toward reticence; thin lips toward quick understanding, full lips
  toward weight.
- **Chin:** a measured chin toward balance, a very fine chin toward
  lightness, a very broad one toward coarseness.
- **The overall shape of the face:** a round, open face toward sociability, a
  hard and angular one toward sharp speech.

## Voice, Speech and Bearing

In firasa the voice carries no fewer signs than the face, because the voice
shows the body's moisture and heat directly.

- Fast, fluent speech → quick grasp; the side of heat.
- A deep, heavy voice → endurance and effort; the side of dryness.
- A nasal voice, speaking from above → a tendency to keep distance.
- A broken or split voice → hesitation, difficulty in trusting.
- Laughing out of place → lightness; never laughing → closure.
- A smiling face and speech in good measure → the balance the tradition holds
  in highest regard.

Gait and bearing enter the same reading: a wide, fast step counts toward
outward-turned energy, a narrow and measured one toward self-restraint.

## The Limits of Firasa

This is the limit the tradition sets on itself, and it is the most important
section here.

**A single sign decides nothing.** Firasa is the art of gathering many signs
and drawing a tendency from them; judging on one is contrary to the method
itself. Signs of heat and signs of cold are both present in any person; what
is sought is which predominates.

**A sign is a tendency, not a fate.** Temperament shows what someone inclines
toward, not what they will do. The same temperament is patience in one person
and stubbornness in another — and the difference lies not in the temperament
but in whether the person knows their own.

**The purpose of knowing is not to sort people but to balance oneself.** In
the tradition the aim of firasa is for a person to recognise their own
inclination, quiet what is excessive in it and feed what is short. To judge
someone else with this knowledge is to turn the science on its head.
