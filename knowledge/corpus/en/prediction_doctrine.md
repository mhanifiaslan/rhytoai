# Prediction Doctrine: The Three Scales of Time

## Three Clocks Run at Once

The sky is not one clock; three run at once. Transits are the outer
weather — the wind that hits your face when you step out the door today.
Progressions are the inner season: the sun may be shining outside while
it is winter within. Solar arc is the arc of life: a single slow sweep of
about one degree a year, spread across decades. A good reading never
confuses the three; it knows which clock speaks at which scale. A day's
tension belongs to a transit, a two-year inward turn to the progressed
Moon, the great thresholds of a life to solar arc.

Where the clocks coincide, a period thickens. When the progressed Moon
is changing signs while a solar arc direction perfects and Saturn
transits the same point, that year gets written into the biography.
Signals that are small alone grow large when they stack — the craft of
prediction is not to read magic into one mark but to see the stacking.

## The Solar Return: A Chart for the Year

Each year the Sun returns exactly to its natal longitude; a chart cast
for that moment sets the tone until the next birthday. The year chart
does not replace the birth chart; it is a cloth laid over it for one
year. The natal chart says what is possible; the year chart says what is
on stage this year.

The spine of the reading is threefold. The year's Ascendant is the door
of the year: the face turned toward the world. The house where the Sun
falls in the year chart is the year's stage: whatever life area the
light strikes is where growth is being summoned. The year's Moon is the
emotional keynote: the register the inner voice will speak in all year.

A year chart gives climate, not dates. "You will move in March" does not
come out of a solar return; "home will weigh heavily this year" does.
The return moment itself is computed to the minute — but when the birth
time is unknown the moment can shift by up to half a day, and an honest
reading then never mentions the Ascendant or houses at all, keeping to
planetary themes. What was not measured is not spoken.

## The Progressed Moon: The Inner Season

The day-for-a-year rule is the ground of progression: each day after
birth stands proxy for one year of life. In this advanced chart the
fastest and most talkative body is the Moon: it changes signs roughly
every two and a half years and completes the zodiac in about twenty-
seven or twenty-eight. The progressed Moon's sign is the person's inner
season — not outer events, but what they are hungry for, what feeds
them, which emotional lesson they are in.

The sign-change date is a threshold: the old season's question closes
and a new one opens. The last degree before the change carries the air
of an examination; the first degrees of the new sign, a strangeness not
yet named. These crossings can and should be dated to the month; a claim
of day-precision slides into prophecy.

The angle between progressed Moon and progressed Sun gives the
progressed lunation phase, which tells where one stands in a roughly
thirty-year cycle. The progressed new moon is seed-time: little shows,
but the period's intent is planted there. The waxing phases are building
years; the progressed full moon is culmination and visibility — whatever
was planted flowers, and whatever is missing becomes visible too. The
waning phases are harvest, pruning, and handing over. The dark of the
cycle is fallow ground before the next seed — read it as preparation,
not barrenness.

The progressed Sun changes sign once in thirty years; that is a change
in the climate of identity and is read with the weight of an era. The
progressed Ascendant and MC are advanced by the solar arc method; when
the birth time is unknown neither is computed, and the reading says so
plainly.

## Solar Arc: The Arc of Life

In solar arc the whole chart is turned together by the distance the
progressed Sun has travelled since birth — about one degree a year. The
rule is austere: one degree, one year. The year a directed planet
perfects a major aspect to a natal point is the year that theme touches
the biography. A half-degree orb opens a window of roughly six months
around the perfection; solar arc dates are therefore spoken in years and
months, never in days.

A solar arc event earns the word "event" without naming the form of the
event. Directed Saturn square the natal Moon may be lived as a move, a
separation, a weight of responsibility, or a long-postponed growing-up;
the common denominator is emotional order compressed and rebuilt. The
reading gives the denominator; the user's life writes the scenario.

## Transit Doctrine: Applying, Exact, Separating

A transiting aspect has three states. Applying is the building theme:
felt before it is named. The day of perfection is the theme at its most
visible and loudest. Separating is digestion: what happened has
happened, and now its meaning settles. The same aspect is therefore read
as "prepare" before perfection and "gather" after it.

The slow planets are the markers of eras. Jupiter enlarges a doorway,
Saturn tests a structure, Uranus opens it from the unexpected side,
Neptune dissolves an edge, Pluto tears out a foundation and pours it
anew, Chiron touches an old wound. Fast-planet transits are daily
weather: not calendar entries but the colour of a single day. The
transiting Moon perfects several aspects in one day; putting it on a
calendar mistakes noise for data.

In retrograde seasons a transit may touch the same point three times:
the first pass opens the matter, the retrograde pass turns it inward for
review, the final pass seals it. Station days — when a planet stands
still to change direction — are the days its theme is most concentrated;
as speed falls to zero, effect rises to its peak.

## The Ethics of Predictive Language

Prediction is not prophecy. The sky speaks in tendencies and writes no
scripts; a reading says "this theme is ripening", not "this will
happen". When dates are given, they are given at the precision the
calculation actually supports: a transit perfection to the day, solar
arc to the month, a year chart to the season. Adding certainty the
mathematics did not produce is not ornament but forgery.

A hard period is called hard — together with its function. A Saturn
square is a tiring examination and at the same time a gate of mastery;
a reading that says only one of these either sells fear or lulls to
sleep. Fear-selling is the oldest corruption of the predictive art:
anxiety breeds dependence, and dependence breeds customers. Honest
prediction turns the user into someone who reads their own calendar —
not someone who watches their fortune-teller.
