# Family Bonds — The Doctrine of the Parent and Sibling Axis

The classical text places the parent and the sibling in separate
corners of the chart. Rytho keeps that placement but does not carry
the source's claims of quantity or death — it reads only **relational
style**: what kind of bond a person formed with a parent, what kind of
closeness or rivalry they lived with a sibling.

## Father and Mother: The Lights and Their Attendants

The doctrine ties parents directly to two planets and the two lights:

- **Father**: the Sun and Saturn.
- **Mother**: the Moon and Venus.

This is not an identity match but a **language of character**. The
condition of the Sun and Saturn in the natal chart — which sign, which
aspects, strong or weak — becomes the language of how a person
experienced the father figure; the Moon and Venus do the same work for
the mother figure.

**Strong, well-witnessed lights** (in their own sign, in exaltation,
aspected by benefics): the parent's presence is experienced as
stability and support.

**Weak or isolated lights** (in fall, unwitnessed, aspected by
malefics): the bond with the parent is experienced as distant,
interrupted, or carrying weight — this is not an accusation, it's a
description of a pattern.

The **relationship between the two lights** says something separate:
how the Sun and Moon converse in the chart is a trace of how the
person INTERNALIZED the dynamic between mother and father — not the
parents' actual relationship, but how the child carries it.

## Siblings: The House of the Moon or Venus

The classical text ties the sibling axis to the maternal axis: by day,
the house holding Venus (and its successor); by night, the house
holding the Moon. The logic: siblinghood is the astrological reflection
of coming from the same source.

If **benefic planets** (Jupiter, Venus) sit in this house or its
neighbor, the sibling bond reads as supportive and warm. If
**malefic planets** (Mars, Saturn) sit there, the bond reads as
competitive or distant — again, a tendency, not an accusation.

The source text's specific claims about the number of siblings or
their sex are **not carried over** — that is a prediction claim and
cannot be drawn from a birth chart.

## The Bond With One's Own Children: The Parent's Other Face

The bond a person will form with their own children is read through
the Midheaven and its successor house — in the classical text this
house is called that of the "good daemon," in today's language the
area of **creativity and what one leaves behind.**

Here **the Moon, Jupiter and Venus** read as giving, **the Sun, Mars
and Saturn** as withholding — but this does not mean "will/won't have
children." Rytho says only this on this axis: what style a person
carries the parenting role in — warm and abundant, or distant and
conditional.

## Boundary — what this doctrine does NOT say

The source text's three chapters (Parents, Siblings, Children) carry
extensive predictions of death, injury, health, and fertility. NONE of
these are carried over:

- **A parent's death, its manner or timing is never interpreted.** The
  source text works through this in detail; Rytho never reads those
  lines.
- **The number, sex, or fate of siblings is never predicted.**
- **Whether someone will have children, fertility, a child's health, or
  lifespan is NEVER interpreted under any circumstance.** This follows
  the same principle as the pregnancy safety gate (see safety rules) —
  it is medicine's domain, not the stars'.
- **No verdict of "good parent / bad parent" is given.** The doctrine
  describes style, not character judgment.

This heading's function in Rytho: to make visible how a person forms
attachment within the family — with a parent, with a sibling, with
their own child — without ever passing verdict on who anyone is.
