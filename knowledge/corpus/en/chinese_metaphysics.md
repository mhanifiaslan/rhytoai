# Chinese Metaphysics: BaZi, I Ching and Mian Xiang

## BaZi (Four Pillars of Destiny)

BaZi ("Eight Characters") derives a four-column chart from the year, month, day
and hour of birth. Each pillar pairs one of the ten Heavenly Stems with one of
the twelve Earthly Branches (the zodiac animals).

- **Day Master**: the Heavenly Stem of the birth day. It names the person's
  core element and basic nature. Yang Fire (Bing) is generous and visible like
  the sun; Yin Water (Gui) is seeping and intuitive like rain.
- **Ten Gods**: the elemental relationship between the Day Master and the other
  stems — resource (learning, protection), output (creativity, expression),
  wealth (opportunity, accumulation), authority (discipline, status), and
  parallel (friendship, rivalry).
- **Luck Pillars (Da Yun)**: ten-year macro periods. The stem and branch of each
  period give that decade its dominant theme.
- **Element balance**: how the five elements (Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water)
  are distributed across the chart. A missing element marks the area a person
  must cultivate deliberately.

Element cycles — Generating: Water→Wood→Fire→Earth→Metal→Water. Controlling:
Wood→Earth→Water→Fire→Metal→Wood. A chart is read through these two cycles
rather than through single placements.

## I Ching (Book of Changes)

The I Ching answers a question with one of 64 hexagrams, each built from two
trigrams of three lines. A line is either yang (unbroken) or yin (broken).

- **Casting**: three coins per line, six times, building the hexagram from the
  bottom up. Certain totals produce a *moving line* — a line in the process of
  turning into its opposite.
- **Moving lines** matter more than the hexagram itself. They mark where the
  situation is already changing and where the questioner has leverage.
- **Transformed hexagram**: flipping the moving lines yields a second hexagram,
  read as the direction the situation is heading.
- The oracle describes a *tendency*, not a verdict. Its classical framing is
  advisory: it tells the questioner what posture the moment rewards.

## Mian Xiang (Face Reading)

Mian Xiang reads temperament and life pattern from facial structure.

- **San Ting (Three Divisions)**: upper (hairline to brows) for early life and
  inherited fortune; middle (brows to nose tip) for middle years and
  self-determination; lower (nose tip to chin) for later years and legacy.
  Balance among the three matters more than any single division.
- **Wu Guan (Five Features)**: brows, eyes, nose, mouth, ears — each governing
  a different faculty, from judgment to vitality to communication.
- **Twelve Palaces**: regions of the face mapped to life domains — career,
  wealth, relationships, siblings, children, and so on.
- **Wu Xing types**: faces classified by dominant element. Wood faces are long
  and upright; Fire faces taper; Earth faces are broad and grounded; Metal
  faces are square and defined; Water faces are round and full.

The tradition treats the face as a record of habit, not a fixed sentence: a
disposition read in the face describes tendency, and tendency can be worked on.
