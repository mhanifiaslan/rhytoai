# Livelihood — The Doctrine of Earning Style

Classical texts call this heading "the fortune of wealth" and usually
chase the question "will they be rich." **Rytho does not ask that
question.** The doctrine here isn't fortune-telling but description: how
a person relates to money, which door their income tends to come
through, and which pattern repeats.

This distinction isn't arbitrary. Quantity can't be measured; **style**
can — because which planet governs the place of livelihood is a fact
sitting in the chart.

## The Part of Fortune: the Marker of Livelihood

Ptolemy ties the question of livelihood to a single point: the **Part
of Fortune**. Its calculation is simple and symmetrical — as far from
the Ascendant as the Sun is from the Moon.

The point itself says nothing; its **ruler** does. Whichever sign holds
the Part of Fortune, livelihood flows from that sign's ruling planet's
nature.

A strong ruler (in its own sign, in exaltation, aspected, visible) means
a steady flow. A weak ruler (in fall, unaspected, combust) doesn't mean
"less" — it means **unstable**. Conflating the two is the most common
trap in reading classical texts.

## The Earning Gate, by Planet

Ptolemy assigns the ruler of livelihood to five planets. The
correspondences below are the core of the doctrine, translated into
today's language without changing the judgment.

**Saturn** — structure, land, time. Earnings accumulate slowly and last:
construction, property, agriculture, long-term commitment, work that
demands patience. Someone whose livelihood is ruled by Saturn is
uneasy with fast money; income that arrives without visible labor
doesn't feel like theirs. The strain: scarcity-fear that doesn't
lift even in real abundance.

**Jupiter** — institution, trust, office. Earnings come from a
position, a role, from inside an institution; they grow as people
extend trust. The strain: appetite for expansion can overreach — the
feeling that "the bigger version is coming" shrinks what's already
in hand.

**Mars** — initiative, risk, edge. Earnings come through contest,
competition, quick moves. Someone whose livelihood is ruled by Mars
loses in stillness and gains in motion. The strain: spending as fast
as earning, and mistaking risk for excitement.

**Venus** — relationship, aesthetics, reciprocity. Earnings come
through people: partnership, consultancy, work built on taste and
harmony, gifts and support. The strain: discomfort talking about
money — not negotiating, not asking to be paid what the work is
worth.

**Mercury** — trade, information, circulation. Earnings come through
commerce, brokering, writing, teaching, craft; income flows through
multiple channels at once. The strain: scattering — three things
running at once, none going deep.

**The Moon and the Sun** aren't earning-rulers on their own, but when
they witness the Part of Fortune they sharpen the picture: the Moon
adds fluctuation (income irregular but recurring in a rhythm), the Sun
adds visibility (earnings become associated with the person's name).

## Inheritance and What Comes From Outside

Ptolemy flags a specific combination: when Saturn, ruling livelihood,
connects with Jupiter, earnings arrive **from outside the person's own
labor** — inheritance, transfer, something left behind. When this bond
forms in the upper angles, what arrives can come from someone with no
blood tie at all.

What the doctrine is really saying here isn't "inheritance" — it's:
**not every income originates from a person's own hand.** In some
charts, the axis of livelihood is give-and-receive, not production.

## Permanence

The classical distinction isn't "much/little" — it's **"holds / doesn't
hold."**

- If stars of the same nature as the ruler of the Part of Fortune also
  give testimony, earnings **settle**.
- If stars of an opposing nature rise above or follow the ruling
  points, earnings **arrive but don't stick.**

This is a structural marker independent of spending habits, and it
names exactly what someone means when they say "I keep making the same
mistake with money" — the mistake isn't a character flaw, it's a
recurring pattern.

## The Second-House Tradition

The post-Ptolemaic tradition (Abu Ma'shar, al-Biruni, and those who
follow them) ties livelihood to the second house: what a person
**earns and controls by their own hand.** The eighth house stands
opposite it: someone else's resource, shared assets, debt and
transfer.

The axis of these two houses asks one question: does a person draw
their livelihood from their own production, or move through someone
else's resource? Both are legitimate; the friction comes from mixing
them.

The ruler of the second house, and what sits inside it, either
confirms or forks what the Part of Fortune says. When it forks, the
doctrine says this: a person's **way of earning** and their **way of
holding onto money** differ, and that gap is the source of the
exhaustion.

## Boundary — what this doctrine does NOT say

The source text carries claims of quantity, degree of wealth, and
timing. These are deliberately not carried over and will not be:

- **How much someone will earn is not stated.** "You will be rich" is
  not a measurement.
- **No investment, trading or debt decision is interpreted.** That is a
  professional's domain; leaning on the sky for it would harm the
  reader.
- **No dated prediction of earnings is made.** Tendency, theme, and
  window are what's offered.
- **No verdict of poverty is given.** The classical text's moral
  vocabulary belongs to its century; the observation is carried, the
  demeaning judgment is not.

This heading's function in Rytho, in one sentence: to make visible the
**style** and the recurring **pattern** of a person's relationship with
money — not their future.
